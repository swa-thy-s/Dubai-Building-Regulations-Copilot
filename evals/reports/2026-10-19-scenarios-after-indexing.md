**Q:** I'm designing an office fit-out in Dubai. Which building regulations and technical standards should I check before finalising my design?

**A:** For an office fit-out in Dubai, check the Dubai Building Code (DBC) 2021, which unifies the building design regulations of Dubai Municipality, DDA, Trakhees and DSOA, and incorporates or cross-references DEWA and IACAD regulations [1][2]. The DBC and the UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice (UAE FLSC) together form the main building design regulations, with the UAE FLSC cross-referenced throughout [3]. Within the DBC, check Part G and Part K for electrical design (DEWA Regulations for Electrical Installations are incorporated there), Part J for security design (SIRA regulations), G.11 for telecommunications (TRA federal regulations), and Part C for accessibility (Dubai Universal Design Code requirements) [2][3][1]. Also check the international codes and standards listed as 'Essential references' in each Part, which are requirements of the DBC — e.g., ASCE/SEI 7-16 for design loads [4]. Where conflicts arise, the most restrictive/highest performance requirement applies [3]. For offices (Business occupancy), the minimum clear height is 2.5 m [5]. A fit-out is defined as a 'Modification' under the DBC [6]. The clauses do not cover approval procedures, so no statement about permits or submission steps is made.

## Q1.2  ANSWERED  (2808 ms)

**Q:** What are the applicable requirements for minimum corridor widths and exit access in an office building? Please cite the exact clauses and document editions.

**A:** For an office building, the minimum clear corridor width is 1.5 m for single loaded corridors and 1.8 m for double loaded corridors [1]. Where a corridor forms part of an accessible route, it must also meet the requirements of C.5 [2]. Exit access corridors must be 1 h fire resistance rated when required by Table 1.11a, Ch. 1 of the UAE FLSC, and exit passageways must achieve the fire resistance rating and construction requirements of Table 3.8, Ch. 3 of the UAE FLSC [3]. The minimum number of exit stairways, stairway width, separation, exit discharge and fire resistance rating are determined in accordance with Ch. 3 of the UAE FLSC, and at least one protected exit stairway shall extend to all roofs [4]. The clear distance between two door openings shall be not less than 1.2 m, and door clear widths shall meet Table B.7: 915 mm for general entry doors and 815 mm for bathrooms and toilets [5][6].

Conditions: Corridor width depends on loading: 1.5 m for single loaded corridors, 1.8 m for double loaded corridors (office occupancy). | The 1 h fire resistance rating for exit access corridors applies only when required by Table 1.11a, Ch. 1 of the UAE FLSC. | Corridors that form part of an accessible route must additionally comply with section C.5. | At least one protected exit stairway must extend to all roofs.

**Q:** I'm planning a fit-out for a retail unit in Dubai. What approvals might I need before starting work, and which authority or building management should I contact?

**A:** For a retail fit-out in Dubai, the landlord must provide you with the approvals required to be submitted to the competent official entities, provided the works do not affect the structure and you hold the official documents requesting them [1]. If the unit is in jointly owned property, you may not make alterations affecting the structure or external appearance without permission under the Owners Association's Articles of Association and Master Community Declaration, and the Owners Association can require repair of resulting damage at your expense [2]. A fit-out is classed as a 'Modification' under the Dubai Building Code, so the DBC and the UAE Fire and Life Safety Code apply to the fit-out [3]. The DBC unifies the building-design regulations of Dubai Municipality, DDA, Trakhees and DSOA, and incorporates or cross-references DEWA, IACAD, SIRA, TRA and Ministry of Interior Civil Defence requirements; permitting procedures for non-building elements remain with the relevant Authority [4]. These clauses do not set out the approval procedure itself.

Conditions: Law No. (26) of 2007, Article 18 applies only where the works do not affect the structure of the real property and the tenant has the official documents requesting the approvals. | Law No. (27) of 2007, Article 23 applies to jointly owned real property; alterations to the structure or external appearance require permission under the Owners Association's Articles of Association and the Master Community Declaration. | Dubai Building Code Table A.1: a fit-out is a 'Modification'; the DBC and UAE FLSC apply to the fit-out, and the approval procedure itself is not covered by these clauses.

## Q3.1  ANSWERED  (3986 ms)

**Q:** What is Al Sa'fat, and how do I determine which requirements apply to my commercial project in Dubai?

**A:** Al Sa'fat is Dubai Municipality's Green Building System (2nd ed. 2023), setting green building rules across Silver, Golden and Platinum Sa'fa levels [1][2]. To find the requirements for your commercial project, classify the building by typology: offices, restaurants/food outlets, hotels, laboratories, resorts and furnished apartments are Residential/Commercial buildings; retail outlets, shopping malls, banks, cinemas/theatres and petrol stations are Public Buildings [3]. In mixed-use buildings, each portion must comply with the regulations for its own typology [4]. For energy and water you may use the Elemental Method (comply with every listed regulation) or the Performance Method (a calculation route; for energy, dynamic thermal modelling against an equal reference building per ASHRAE 90.1 Appendix G) [4][5]. Golden Sa'fa includes all mandatory and optional Silver Sa'fa requirements plus more [2]. For Golden/Platinum Sa'fa and all new buildings other than villas, a performance report covering at least 6 months, including 3 thermal peak months, must be submitted [6].

Conditions: Mixed-use buildings: each portion must comply with the regulations for its particular typology. | Change of use: the regulations apply for the new use. | Energy Performance Method: the proposed building's annual energy consumption must be equal to or lower than an equal reference building per ASHRAE 90.1 Appendix G. | Water Performance Method: may be used where the building does not comply with the elemental requirements for water efficient fixtures in Article 601.01. | Regulation 503.07 performance report applies to Golden and Platinum Sa'fa and all new buildings other than villas.

Sources: [1] Al Sa'fat Dubai Green Building System 2nd ed. 2023 Front matter p25 p25; [2] Al Sa'fat Dubai Green Building System 2nd ed. 2023 Front matter p31 p31; [3] Al Sa'fat Dubai Green Building System 2nd ed. 2023 Front matter p21 p21; [4] Al Sa'fat Dubai Green Building System 2nd ed. 2023 Front matter p22 p22; [5] Al Sa'fat Dubai Green Building System 2nd ed. 2023 Front matter p23 p23; [6] Al Sa'fat Dubai Green Building System 2nd ed. 2023 503.07 p62

**Q:** Are there requirements for construction materials, recycled content, or low-emission finishes? Separate mandatory requirements from voluntary sustainability recommendations.

**A:** Yes. Dubai has mandatory material requirements, plus a higher recycled-content target for top green-building tiers. Mandatory for all new buildings: recycled content of at least 10% of total material value per Dubai Building Code B.10.6.1 [1]; insulation materials must be CFC-free, non-toxic, contain 0.05 ppm or less added formaldehyde, have a TLV of 0.1 or less of individual VOCs, be fire resistant per Dubai Civil Defence, and be certified by Dubai Central Laboratory [6]; lead/heavy-metal rules in B.10.6.4 and villa part K.5.10.2 apply [5]. Low-emitting finishes: paints, coatings, adhesives and sealants should not exceed Dubai Municipality VOC limits and must be certified by Dubai Central Laboratory or an approved laboratory [3][4].

Conditions: The 10% recycled-content calculation excludes mechanical, electrical and plumbing components, furniture and specialty items such as elevators; only permanently installed materials count, and partially recycled products contribute only their recycled percentage by weight. | The 15% recycled-content requirement applies only to Golden and Platinum Sa'fa buildings; all new buildings must at least meet the 10% Dubai Building Code requirement. | Insulation material requirements apply to all new buildings; villas must additionally follow part K (K.10.1.6.5 CFC-free materials, K.5.10.2 lead/heavy metals, K.10.1.6.3 indoor air quality). | Low-emitting material provisions apply to all buildings, including new applications in existing buildings.

## Q3.4  ANSWERED  (4179 ms)

**Q:** My project is an existing office refurbishment rather than a new building. Do the same requirements apply? Show the source supporting your conclusion.

**A:** Yes, the Dubai Building Code applies to your existing office refurbishment, but the specific requirements depend on the type of change. The DBC applies to renovation, modification, reconstruction, addition to, or change of use of an existing building [1]. Table A.1 classifies changes: renovation (linings/finishes or structural strengthening, no reconfiguration), modification (fit-out or change to a service system/element), reconstruction (affects adjacent egress), change of use, and addition; the DBC and UAE FLSC apply to the change and any impacted areas [2]. For an office refurbishment, if it is a fit-out or service change it is a modification; if only finishes, it is a renovation. Al Sa'fat has separate existing-building requirements: IAQ compliance for listed building types (hotels, malls, etc.) and re-commissioning for buildings with cooling load ≥2 MW [3][4].

Conditions: The DBC applies to existing buildings; the applicable code depends on whether the work is renovation, modification, reconstruction, addition, or change of use. | Al Sa'fat existing-building IAQ requirements apply to listed building types or as determined by DM; re-commissioning applies to existing buildings with cooling load ≥2 MW.

## Q5.1  ANSWERED  (5872 ms)

**Q:** I want to renovate my apartment in Dubai. Do I need approval to remove an internal wall?

**A:** Yes. Removing an internal wall is a structural alteration, not a "renovation" under the Dubai Building Code, which excludes any reconfiguration of internal spaces [1]. Under Law No. (27) of 2007, Article 23, a unit owner or occupier may not make alterations to the structure of the unit that would materially affect it or the jointly owned property unless permitted by the Owners Association's Articles of Association and Master Community Declaration [4]. The DBC classifies such work as a "Modification" (change to a building element), or "Reconstruction" if it affects adjacent egress routes or systems [2][3]; the DBC clauses describe which codes apply but do not cover the approval procedure. If the wall is a dividing wall between units that forms part of the Common Areas, it is shared by both owners [5]. For tenants, Article 18 of Law No. (26) of 2007 covers only works that do not affect the structure [6].

Conditions: If the wall is a dividing wall between adjacent units that forms part of the Common Areas, it is shared by both owners (Law No. 27 of 2007, Article 8). | If the wall is a non-load bearing wall inside the unit, it forms part of the unit (Law No. 27 of 2007, Article 8). | If the work affects adjacent egress routes or systems beyond the work area, it is classified as Reconstruction under DBC Table A.1. | For tenants, the landlord's obligation to provide approvals under Law No. 26 of 2007, Article 18 applies only to works that do not affect the structure, so it does not cover removing a wall.

**Q:** Who is responsible for approving this work: the relevant government authority, the building management, or both? Please distinguish legal requirements from building-specific procedures.

**A:** Both may be involved, depending on the work. For a tenant's decoration works that do not affect the structure, the landlord must provide the approvals required to be submitted to the competent official entities [1]. For a unit in jointly owned property, alterations affecting the structure or external appearance require permission under the Owners Association's Articles of Association and Master Community Declaration [2]; the Owners Association is responsible for managing the common areas [3]. The Dubai Building Code and Al Sa'fat clauses only state which regulations apply to renovation, modification, reconstruction, change of use, or addition [4], and that Dubai Municipality may set special standards for historical or heritage buildings [5]; they do not cover the approval procedure.

Conditions: Article 18 applies to tenants carrying out decoration works or other works that do not affect the structure of the real property and where the tenant has the official documents requesting the approvals. | Article 23 applies to unit owners or occupiers in jointly owned property making alterations that materially affect the unit, the jointly owned property, or its external appearance. | DBC Table A.1 applicability depends on the type of change: renovation, modification, reconstruction, change of use, or addition.
